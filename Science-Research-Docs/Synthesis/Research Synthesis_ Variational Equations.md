

# **Geometric and Algebraic Transformations of Variational Equations in the Three-Body Problem: A Synthesis of Integrability Obstructions and Regularization Strategies**

## **I. The Galoisian Obstruction: A Precise Formulation of the Integrability Problem**

The distinguishing boundary between integrable and chaotic behavior in Hamiltonian dynamics remains one of the most profound challenges in mathematical physics. This research project is predicated on the rigorous application of differential Galois theory to analyze, and potentially circumvent, the obstructions to integrability in the classical Three-Body Problem (3BP). By translating the analytic properties of dynamical systems into the algebraic properties of their linear approximations, we establish a unified framework for understanding why the 3BP resists classical solution methods and how modern geometric transformations might reveal hidden structures within its chaotic veneer.

### **A. The Morales-Ramis-Simó Criterion for Liouville Integrability**

The fundamental concept of complete integrability for a Hamiltonian system $X\_H$ defined on a complex analytic symplectic manifold $M$ of dimension $2n$ is articulated in the Liouville sense. A system is designated as Liouville integrable if it admits $n$ functionally independent meromorphic first integrals that are in involution—meaning their pairwise Poisson brackets vanish. The existence of these integrals implies that the phase space is foliated by invariant tori, upon which the motion is quasi-periodic. The Morales-Ramis theory provides the most potent algebraic criterion for determining the existence of these integrals by connecting the global analytic properties of the system to the algebraic structure of its variational equations (VEs) along a specific solution.

Let $\\phi(t)$ be a particular non-equilibrium solution of $X\_H$, which defines a complex analytic curve $\\Gamma$, often a Riemann surface, within the phase space.1 The linearization of the vector field along this trajectory yields the Variational Equation, a system of linear differential equations governing the evolution of infinitesimal displacements $y$ from the reference orbit:

$$\\dot{y} \= A(t)y \\quad \\text{where} \\quad A(t) \= \\frac{\\partial X\_H}{\\partial x}(\\phi(t))$$  
The coefficients of the matrix $A(t)$ are meromorphic functions on the curve $\\Gamma$. The structural analysis of this equation relies on the Differential Galois Group (DGG), denoted as $G$. The DGG is the group of differential automorphisms of the Picard-Vessiot extension—the smallest differential field extension of the base field containing all linearly independent solutions to the VE. Because the original system is Hamiltonian, the DGG is a linear algebraic subgroup of the symplectic group $Sp(2n, \\mathbb{C})$.

The celebrated Morales-Ramis Theorem establishes a necessary condition for Liouville integrability: if a Hamiltonian system is meromorphically integrable in the Liouville sense, then the identity component $G^\\circ$ of the differential Galois group of the Variational Equation along $\\Gamma$ must be Abelian.1 The identity component $G^\\circ$ is the largest connected algebraic subgroup containing the identity element. This theorem provides a "litmus test" for non-integrability; finding a non-abelian identity component for the VE of any single solution is sufficient to prove the non-integrability of the global system.

The theory was subsequently extended by Morales, Ramis, and Simó to consider higher-order variational equations. The Morales-Ramis-Simó theorem asserts that for a system to be integrable, the identity component of the DGGs for variational equations of all orders $k \\geq 1$ must be Abelian.1 This extension is crucial for systems where the first-order VE might deceptively yield an Abelian group (due to simplified local dynamics), while the non-integrability manifests only in the non-linear terms captured by higher-order variations.3

### **B. The Non-Abelian Baseline in Three-Body Dynamics**

The premise of this project rests on the established fact that the general three-body problem possesses a non-abelian DGG, thereby confirming its meromorphic non-integrability. The literature provides a robust catalogue of specific orbital configurations where this obstruction has been rigorously computed.

**The Planar Three-Body Problem:** Extensive research by Maciejewski and Przybylska has demonstrated that the planar three-body problem, when restricted to a common level of energy and angular momentum, does not admit an additional meromorphic first integral.5 Their analysis utilized the monodromy group—a subgroup of the DGG generated by analytic continuation of solutions around singularities in the complex time plane. Along specific parabolic orbits, the monodromy group was shown to be non-abelian, precluding the existence of the necessary rational invariants for integrability.

**Lagrange’s Equilateral Orbit:** The variational equations normal to the celebrated Lagrange equilateral solution (where the three bodies form a rotating equilateral triangle) have been exhaustively studied. The DGG for the normal variational equation (NVE) along this orbit is isomorphic to $SL(2, \\mathbb{C})$ or a related non-abelian subgroup.1 The dynamics of the normal oscillations involve elliptic or hypergeometric functions, which yield a rich, non-solvable monodromy structure. Since $SL(2, \\mathbb{C})$ is not an Abelian group, this finding rigorously rules out Liouville integrability for the system in the neighborhood of the Lagrangian points.

**Collinear Euler Orbits:** Similarly, the DGG around the collinear Euler orbits (where the three bodies remain on a line) has been proven to be non-abelian. Combot extended this analysis to higher-order variational equations, computing terms up to the 5th order to confirm the meromorphic non-integrability of the collinear 3-body problem for positive masses.1 This result is particularly significant as it demonstrates that the obstruction persists even when considering the non-linear expansion of the dynamics.

**The Restricted Three-Body Problem (R3BP):** The non-integrability of the Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem has been re-confirmed with modern algebraic precision. Yagasaki applied the Morales-Ramis-Simó theory to the CR3BP, proving non-integrability for any non-zero secondary mass.7 The analysis focused on resonant periodic orbits, showing that the DGG of the associated variational equations remains non-abelian. This aligns with Poincaré's classical divergence of series results but places them on a strictly algebraic footing.

These findings establish the "input" for the project: a set of dynamical systems where $G^\\circ$ is known to be non-abelian. The scientific objective is not to re-prove this well-known non-integrability, but to identify specific transformations that can map these systems to a new regime where the Galoisian obstruction is removed.

### **C. Distinctions in Integrability Definitions**

To navigate the landscape of transformations effectively, one must distinguish between Liouville integrability and the weaker concept of integrability by quadratures. The Morales-Ramis condition for Liouville integrability requires an Abelian $G^\\circ$. However, a system is said to be integrable by quadratures if the solution field of the VE is a Liouvillian extension. According to the Kolchin theory of differential groups, this corresponds to a Solvable $G^\\circ$.1

Since any Abelian group is Solvable, Liouville integrability implies integrability by quadratures, but the converse is not true. A transformation that reduces a non-solvable group (like $SL(2, \\mathbb{C})$) to a solvable, non-abelian group (e.g., the group of upper triangular matrices) would render the VE solvable by quadratures. This is a significant partial success: while it does not guarantee the full system is Liouville integrable with commuting integrals, it implies the solutions can be constructed via a tower of exponentials and integrals. The project aims to classify transformations that achieve either the Solvable or the Abelian state, viewing "solvability" as a stepping stone toward full "abelianization."

| Integrability Type | Definition | DGG (G∘) Constraint | Relationship |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Liouville Integrability** | Existence of $n$ independent, commuting meromorphic integrals. | **Abelian** | Strongest Condition |
| **Integrability by Quadratures** | Solutions expressible via finite tower of integrals, exponentials, and algebraics. | **Solvable** | Intermediate Condition |
| **B-Integrability** | Bogoyavlenskij integrability (preservation of a flag of distributions). | **Virtually Abelian** | Generalized Condition (Non-Hamiltonian) |

## **II. Taxonomy of Transformations: From Invariance to Abelianization**

The core innovation of this research lies in classifying transformations based on their effect on the DGG. The analysis reveals that standard coordinate manipulations are insufficient, necessitating deeper algebraic and geometric interventions to modify the fundamental structure of the differential module.

### **A. Class 0: Invariant Transformations (Ineffective)**

The research validates that "obvious" transformations often fail to remove the integrability obstruction because the property of the DGG being non-abelian is intrinsic to the system's differential module.

**Symplectic Coordinate Changes:** A meromorphic symplectic change of coordinates $\\Phi$ on the phase space transforms the VE $\\dot{y} \= A(t)y$ into a new system $\\dot{z} \= B(t)z$. However, mathematically, this transformation represents a gauge equivalence over the base differential field $k \= M(\\Gamma)$.1 The relationship between the matrices is given by $B(t) \= P(t)^{-1}A(t)P(t) \- P(t)^{-1}\\dot{P}(t)$, where $P$ is the Jacobian of the transformation. Since the DGG is an invariant of the underlying differential module (the abstract vector space with connection), it is, by definition, invariant under gauge transformations. Consequently, the non-abelian nature of $G^\\circ$ is preserved. Symplectic coordinate changes are therefore ineffective for "abelianization".1

**Algebraic Time Reparametrizations:** A general algebraic change of the independent variable $t \= f(\\tau)$ changes the base differential field from $k$ to $k'$. However, the solution field $L$ (the Picard-Vessiot extension) remains the same, as the solutions are the same analytic functions merely composed with $f(\\tau)$.1 The algebraic structure of $G^\\circ$ reflects the geometric branching and monodromy of the solutions in the complex plane, which are topological properties invariant under the speed at which the trajectory is traversed.1 Thus, simple time reparametrizations do not alter the group structure.

### **B. Class 1: Algebraic Transformations (Field Extensions)**

Effective "abelianization" begins with transformations that modify the base field itself, specifically through algebraic extensions.

**Algebraic Coverings:** The application of a ramified covering of the independent variable, such as $t \= s^m$ ($m \> 1$), constitutes a finite algebraic extension of the base field $k$ to a new field $k\_s$.1 The DGG of the transformed system over the new base field, $G\_s \= \\text{Gal}(L\_s/k\_s)$, is an algebraic subgroup of the original DGG $G$. This process is known as algebrization. By moving to the covering surface (a Riemann surface of higher genus), one can "unwind" the monodromy around singular points. For example, a loop around a singularity that generated a non-trivial monodromy matrix in the original base field might become a closed loop with identity monodromy in the covering field.1

It is theoretically possible for a non-abelian $G^\\circ$ to possess an abelian subgroup $G\_s^\\circ$. Therefore, finding the specific covering degree $m$ that induces this reduction is a viable algebraic strategy. This does not "create" integrability out of nothing but rather isolates a subsystem or a specific perspective where the dynamics appear regular.

### **C. Class 2: Geometric Equivalence (Eisenhart Lifts)**

The Eisenhart-Duval lift represents a geometric transformation that reframes the algebraic obstruction. This method lifts a time-dependent Hamiltonian system with potential $V(q)$ on configuration space $M$ to a geodesic flow on a Lorentzian manifold $M\_E$ of dimension $\\dim(M) \+ 2$. The metric on the extended space involves the potential $V$ as a component of the metric tensor.1

The critical insight validated by the research is the equivalence between the Liouville integrability of the original potential $V$ and the existence of Killing tensors on the Eisenhart manifold $M\_E$.1 Specifically, the condition for the VE to have an abelian $G^\\circ$ corresponds directly to the geometric symmetries of the lifted space. In this framework, the "transformation" is conceptual: the problem of finding an abelian DGG is mapped to the problem of finding Killing tensors (isometries) in a higher dimension. If the 3BP is non-integrable, the Eisenhart lift implies a specific lack of irreducible conformal Killing tensors in the Lorentzian lift. This transformation is valuable because techniques for finding Killing tensors (e.g., utilizing the Schouten bracket) differ from techniques for solving differential equations.

### **D. Class 3: Geometric Parallelism (Jacobi-Maupertuis)**

The Jacobi-Maupertuis (JM) principle transforms a Hamiltonian flow on a constant energy surface $H=E$ into a geodesic flow on a Riemannian manifold equipped with the Jacobi metric $g\_{JM} \= (E \- V(q))g\_0$, where $g\_0$ is the kinetic energy metric.1

Crucially, the research highlights that the VE of the original time-dependent flow and the geodesic deviation equation (Jacobi equation) of the JM flow are *not* equivalent linear systems.1 They govern different vector fields on different manifolds. This constitutes a "parallel" transformation: the original dynamic problem is discarded in favor of a new geometric one. The transformation succeeds if the geodesic flow on the Jacobi metric manifold is integrable (abelian DGG), even if the original system was not. However, for the 3BP, the curvature of the Jacobi metric is highly complex, with prevalent regions of negative sectional curvature indicating geodesic instability and chaos.13 While the JM lift provides a geometric interpretation of chaos, it rarely simplifies the DGG directly unless the energy level $E$ is chosen to correspond to a specific integrable limit.

## **III. Kustaanheimo-Stiefel Regularization: The Prototype of Momentum Map Reduction**

The most significant "Class 4" (Geometric Local) transformation analyzed is the Kustaanheimo-Stiefel (KS) regularization. This transformation is identified as the prototype for how geometric lifts can resolve singularities and potentially reveal hidden symmetries. The research synthesizes the interpretation of KS constraints not merely as algebraic tricks, but as fundamental momentum map conditions within the context of symplectic reduction.

### **A. The Geometry of the KS Lift**

The KS transformation lifts the 3-dimensional Kepler problem (singular at the origin) to a 4-dimensional harmonic oscillator (regular at the origin). The physical phase space $T^\*\\mathbb{R}^3$ is replaced by an extended phase space $\\tilde{M} \= T^\*\\mathbb{R}^4 \\setminus \\{0\\}$.1 This lift introduces a new "fictitious" time variable $s$ (where $dt \= r ds$) and an additional spatial dimension.

The geometric structure underlying this transformation is the Hopf fibration $S^3 \\to S^2$. The extended space $\\mathbb{R}^4 \\setminus \\{0\\}$ projects to the physical space $\\mathbb{R}^3 \\setminus \\{0\\}$ as a principal circle bundle with fiber $S^1$.1 The 4-vector $u$ in the extended space relates to the physical vector $r$ via quadratic forms involving spinors or quaternions. The $S^1$ fiber represents the gauge freedom of the transformation: rotating the 4-vector $u$ by a phase $e^{i\\phi}$ leaves the physical position $r$ invariant.

### **B. Constraints as Momentum Maps**

A pivotal theoretical validation in this report is the identification of the KS algebraic constraints with the zero-level set of a momentum map. The extra $U(1)$ symmetry acting on the fibers is Hamiltonian, possessing a conserved quantity (momentum map) $\\Xi$. The explicit form of this map in coordinates $(u, p\_u)$ corresponds to the "bilinear relation" required to ensure the motion remains on the physical manifold 1:

$$\\Xi(u, p\_u) \= u\_1 p\_{u\_2} \- u\_2 p\_{u\_1} \+ u\_3 p\_{u\_4} \- u\_4 p\_{u\_3} \= 0$$  
Enforcing $\\Xi \= 0$ is geometrically equivalent to restricting the dynamics to the zero-angular-momentum sector of the 4D oscillator in the fiber direction.1 This confirms that the KS regularization fits strictly within the Marsden-Weinstein symplectic reduction framework. The 4D harmonic oscillator is the "upstairs" integrable system; the Kepler problem is the "downstairs" reduced system obtained by quotienting out the $S^1$ symmetry at the $\\Xi=0$ level.16 The "regularization" works because the oscillator is non-singular at the origin, whereas the projection maps this regular origin to the singular origin of the Kepler potential.

### **C. The Emergence of Magnetic Terms and Curvature**

The reduction process explains the complexity of the dynamics "downstairs." When reducing a principal bundle, the symplectic form on the reduced space is modified by a curvature term associated with the mechanical connection.1

$$\\omega\_{\\text{red}} \= \\omega\_{\\text{can}} \- \\mu F\_A$$  
Here, $\\mu$ is the value of the momentum map (typically 0 for KS, but non-zero for generalizations) and $F\_A$ is the curvature of the connection. In the context of KS and Kepler, this curvature manifests as a "magnetic" term—specifically, the field of a magnetic monopole.1 Even though the "upstairs" system (harmonic oscillator) is perfectly integrable and free of magnetic forces, the geometric projection introduces effective forces (Coriolis or Lorentz-like) in the reduced space.

This provides a profound insight into the 3BP: non-integrability in the physical space may be an artifact of the projection. The "hidden" symmetries might exist in the lifted bundle but are obscured by the non-trivial curvature (holonomy) introduced during reduction.1 The chaotic trajectories of the 3BP could be projections of regular trajectories traversing a bundle with complex curvature.

| **KS Transformation** | $T^\*\\mathbb{R}^4$ | $U(1) \\cong S^1$ | Kepler $\\to$ Harmonic Oscillator via Hopf Fibration |

| **Heggie's Global** | $T^\*\\mathbb{R}^{4(N-1)}$ | $T^{N-1}$ | Regularizes all pairwise collisions via spanning tree |

## **IV. Extensions to N-Body Systems and Bundle Curvature**

Moving beyond the two-body prototype, the research evaluates generalizations of KS regularization to the N-body problem, specifically the Aarseth-Zare and Heggie formulations, analyzing them through the lens of bundle geometry.

### **A. Aarseth-Zare Regularization**

The Aarseth-Zare regularization is designed to handle the general three-body problem by applying a KS transform to a selected binary pair (e.g., bodies 1 and 2\) while treating the third body in standard coordinates. This results in an 8-dimensional phase space for the relative motion.1 The transformation introduces redundant variables and requires closure constraints to maintain physical consistency with the triangle inequality and orientation. While effective for regularizing binary collisions ($R\_{12} \\to 0$), it does not yield a global group-theoretic structure as clean as the single KS map because it privileges one pair. The closure constraints effectively act as complicated momentum map conditions that define a submanifold within the 8D space.1

### **B. Heggie’s Global Regularization**

Heggie extended this approach by chaining multiple KS mappings along a spanning tree of the $N$ bodies.1 This creates a highly extended phase space $T^\*\\mathbb{R}^{4(N-1)}$. For the 3-body problem, this involves regularizing two separation vectors simultaneously. The research suggests interpreting this as a lift to a principal torus bundle ($T^{N-1}$). The independent rotations of each KS spinor constitute a $T^k$ gauge symmetry. The closure constraints again act to couple these fibers.

### **C. The Conjecture of Hidden Symmetries**

The analysis posits that the "chaos" of the 3BP is encoded in the curvature of this higher-dimensional bundle. Just as the Kepler reduction introduces a monopole term, the reduction of the 8D or 12D regularized 3BP space introduces complex, non-abelian curvature terms (Yang-Mills type fields) into the effective Hamiltonian.1 The "upstairs" system in the Heggie regularization might possess symmetries (and thus abelian DGG subgroups) that are broken or hidden by the holonomy of the projection to the physical frame. Specifically, the "hidden integrability" observed in certain large-N limits or specific potentials might be realizable on the bundle space before the "magnetic" curvature terms destroy the commuting integrals.23

## **V. Generalized Integrability: B-Integrability and the Ayoul-Zung Theorem**

A significant theoretical advancement reviewed is the concept of Bogoyavlenskij (B-) integrability and its relation to the Galoisian obstruction. This broadens the scope beyond Hamiltonian systems, allowing for a more flexible definition of regularity.

### **A. Bogoyavlenskij Integrability**

While Liouville integrability demands $n$ first integrals in involution for a $2n$-dimensional system, B-integrability requires the existence of a flag of invariant foliations and symmetries that preserve the system's structure.25 It generalizes integrability to dynamical systems that may not be Hamiltonian or where the symplectic structure is degenerate.

### **B. The Ayoul-Zung Theorem**

The standard Morales-Ramis theorem applies strictly to Liouville integrability. A gap in the earlier literature concerned the Galoisian condition for B-integrability. The Ayoul-Zung theorem fills this gap, generalizing the Morales-Ramis result. It states that if a system is meromorphically B-integrable, the DGG of its variational equations (of any order) must be *virtually abelian*.2 A group is virtually abelian if its identity component is abelian (i.e., its Lie algebra is abelian).

This result is profound for the project. It implies that even if one abandons the strict Hamiltonian formalism (e.g., via non-canonical transformations or dissipative lifts), the target for integrability remains an Abelian DGG. The "virtual" qualification allows for finite non-abelian extensions (component groups), but the continuous symmetries must commute. This provides a rigorous check for any proposed non-Hamiltonian regularization or lift: if the DGG of the lifted system is not virtually abelian, the lift has failed to render the system B-integrable.28

## **VI. Computational Strategy and Machine Learning Applications**

The theoretical framework is complemented by a robust computational strategy designed to validate the existence of hidden invariants in the lifted spaces using high-precision numerics and machine learning.

### **A. High-Precision Symplectic Integration**

To distinguish true chaos from numerical noise over the timescales required to detect subtle invariants, standard double-precision arithmetic (16 digits) is insufficient due to the exponential divergence of trajectories (Lyapunov instability). The project mandates the use of Double-Double (DD) arithmetic, providing approximately 31 decimal digits of precision.1

The integration is performed using the REBOUND framework with the IAS15 integrator (a 15th-order adaptive Gauss-Radau integrator).1 This high-order, symplectic-preserving setup is essential for tracking conserved quantities (like the Jacobi constant) with errors below $10^{-25}$ over $10,000+$ orbits.1 This precision allows for the rigorous computation of chaos indicators like MEGNO (Mean Exponential Growth factor of Nearby Orbits) and FLI (Fast Lyapunov Indicator).32 These indicators serve as "ground truth" labels: if MEGNO converges to 2, the orbit is regular; if it diverges, it is chaotic. The high precision ensures that a diverging MEGNO is due to intrinsic chaos, not round-off error.

### **B. Machine Learning for Symmetry Discovery**

The project employs machine learning not merely as a predictive tool, but as an instrument for mathematical discovery, leveraging "inductive biases" to find conserved quantities.

**Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs):** HNNs are deep learning models designed with an inductive bias that enforces symplectic structure and energy conservation.34 Instead of learning the vector field $\\dot{q}, \\dot{p}$ directly, they learn a scalar function $H\_\\theta(q,p)$ and derive the gradients. By training HNNs on the trajectories of the lifted (regularized) 3BP, the network implicitly learns the "upstairs" Hamiltonian structure. If the lifted system is closer to integrability, the HNN is expected to generalize better and show lower loss than on the Cartesian coordinates.35

**Symplectic Recurrent Neural Networks (SympNets):** These networks consist of layers of symplectic maps, ensuring that the learned transformation is strictly symplectic.36 They are used to learn the flow map of the system over long time steps while preserving geometric invariants.

**Symbolic Regression (AI Feynman & AI Poincaré):** The ultimate goal is to extract explicit analytic forms of the conserved quantities. Algorithms like AI Feynman and AI Poincaré are employed to perform symbolic regression on the latent representations learned by the HNNs.37 The strategy is to train the network to predict the trajectory, then analyze the bottleneck layers or the learned Hamiltonian to see if they depend on fewer variables than the full phase space dimensions (indicating a conservation law). AI Poincaré specifically looks for overlaps in the "shape space" of trajectories to identify the rank of the Poisson manifold and the number of conserved quantities.40 The hypothesis is that while no algebraic integral exists in $(q, p)$, a rational integral might exist in the lifted variables $(u, p\_u)$ or their invariants.41

## **VII. Synthesis: The Inverse Morales-Ramis Framework**

This research culminates in the proposal of an "Inverse Morales-Ramis" framework. While the standard theory acts as a filter (**Integrable $\\implies$ Abelian DGG**), the inverse framework acts as a construction engine: **Construct Lift $\\implies$ Check Abelian DGG $\\implies$ Infer Integrability**.

The strategy weaves together the identified transformation classes into a cohesive pipeline:

1. **Diagnosis:** Compute the DGG of the specific 3-body orbit in Cartesian coordinates. Confirm the non-abelian identity component $G^\\circ$ (e.g., existence of $SL(2, \\mathbb{C})$).  
2. **Regularization (Class 4):** Apply Kustaanheimo-Stiefel or Heggie regularization to lift the system to a principal torus bundle ($T^\*\\mathbb{R}^{4k}$). This removes singularities (collisions), converting the local monodromy from irregular to regular (Algebrization).  
3. **Symmetry Search (ML \+ Class 2):** Use Hamiltonian Neural Networks and AI Poincaré on the lifted trajectories to identify candidate conserved quantities (hidden symmetries) in the higher-dimensional space. The search looks for functions that are constant on the flow but may not be pull-backs of functions on the base space.  
4. **Validation (Class 1):** Check if the DGG of the lifted system, when restricted to the level sets of these new machine-discovered invariants, is Abelian or Solvable. If the HNN finds a new constant, the restricted DGG must collapse to an abelian group.  
5. **Reduction Analysis:** If an Abelian group is found "upstairs," analyze the reduction map. The obstruction to integrability "downstairs" is identified as the curvature term (magnetic field) of the mechanical connection. The "chaos" is physically interpreted as the particle interacting with this non-abelian gauge field.

## **Conclusion**

The research described herein validates that the non-integrability of the Three-Body Problem is fundamentally a manifestation of the algebraic structure of its variational equations, characterized by non-abelian Differential Galois Groups. However, this obstruction is not invariant under all geometric transformations. By lifting the dynamics to principal bundles via Kustaanheimo-Stiefel regularizations, the system is embedded in a higher-dimensional phase space with richer symmetry potential. The algebraic constraints of these regularizations are rigorously identified as momentum map conditions, linking the problem to symplectic reduction theory.

The integration of high-precision numerical analysis and structure-preserving machine learning provides a novel pathway to discovering "hidden" symmetries that reside in the bundle geometry. The Ayoul-Zung theorem ensures that even generalized B-integrability requires a virtually abelian DGG, keeping the target algebraic structure clear. The proposed "Inverse Morales-Ramis" framework offers a systematic method to search for these abelian lifts, potentially transforming the intractability of the 3BP into a problem of geometric gauge theory. This synthesis establishes a scientifically robust foundation for exploring the frontier between chaotic dynamics, differential algebra, and geometric mechanics.
